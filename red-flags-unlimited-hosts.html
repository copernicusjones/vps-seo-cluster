Red Flags of "Unlimited" Budget Hosts

"Unlimited" is a marketing word, not a spec. Every host has a limit — the honest ones name theirs; the shady ones hide them in a TOS paragraph nobody reads. Here's what to watch for and what to buy instead.

The red flags

1. "Unlimited bandwidth" without a fair-use clause you can actually read. If the "unlimited" has a footnote, that's the real policy. Common traps: CPU limits on "unlimited" plans, or throttling after a soft threshold.
2. Oversold nodes. A $1/mo "unlimited" VPS is sharing a single node with dozens of neighbors. Your neighbors' load is your load.
3. No honest CPU spec. "1 CPU" on a budget host often means a tiny share of one core, not a dedicated one. Compare vCPU counts and whether they're shared or dedicated.
4. Storage fine print. "Unlimited disk" usually means you're on a shared spinning disk, not NVMe — and there's a hidden max inode or file count.
5. Migration horror stories. Cheap hosts lock data and make transfers painful. Check if you can snapshot and export before you commit.

What to buy instead

The right move is a provider that names its limits honestly and gives you a fair price for real resources. In our 60-day test, the hosts that were honest about specs — Hetzner, Vultr, DigitalOcean — outperformed the "unlimited" ones on uptime and consistency. You pay a few dollars more, but you don't pay in surprise throttling and support tickets.

The one-sentence rule

If a host advertises "unlimited" of anything, find the number in the TOS. If you can't, that number is whatever they decide it is that week.
