Home Assistant on a VPS (2026)

Can Home Assistant run on a cheap VPS, or should it live on hardware in your home? The honest split.

Bottom line: yes, Home Assistant runs fine on a 2 GB VPS — if your devices are cloud-connected or reachable over the internet. The catch: most smart-home gear (Zigbee, Z-Wave, local LAN devices) needs a USB dongle or local network, which a VPS can't give you. If you use cloud-based devices and want HA as a remote controller, a VPS is a great fit. If you have local radios, keep HA on a box at home.

The hardware split:
VPS works when: your devices are Wi-Fi / cloud / LAN-reachable, you want always-on remote access, and you don't need a Zigbee/Z-Wave USB dongle.
Local box wins when: you need a USB dongle for Zigbee/Z-Wave/Thread, or you want everything to work when your internet goes down.

When a VPS makes sense

  • You use cloud or IP-based devices (many Wi-Fi bulbs, plugs, Tuya, ESPHome over IP).
  • You want always-on remote access from your phone anywhere without a VPN tunnel home.
  • You're comfortable with tailscale or a reverse proxy to reach it securely.
  • You want no hardware on your desk — everything in one cloud box.

The honest tradeoff

The biggest gotcha: Home Assistant's most valuable integrations (local Zigbee/Z-Wave hubs, USB radios) cannot work on a VPS — there's no USB passthrough to a dongle you don't own. If your home is built around those, a VPS for HA is a trap. A good middle path: run HA at home and use a VPS as the always-on reverse proxy / remote entry point, which is often the better architecture anyway.
