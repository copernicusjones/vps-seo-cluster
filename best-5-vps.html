Best $5 VPS in 2026: What You Actually Get

A $5 budget is the sweet spot for a first server. Here's exactly what it buys you and where it stops.

Bottom line: a $5-ish VPS in 2026 reliably gets you 1 GB of RAM, 1 vCPU, and 20–30 GB of NVMe storage — enough for one or two real services like n8n, Uptime Kuma, a personal Docker stack, or a low-traffic site. It is not enough to run Plex transcoding, a big database, or multiple heavy apps at once. If you know that boundary, a $5 box is the best first-server value there is.

What fits comfortably in 1 GB of RAM

  • One low-traffic website (nginx + a static site or PHP-FPM for a small CMS)
  • n8n for automation — runs fine in ~300–500 MB for modest workflows
  • Uptime Kuma — idles around 100–150 MB with dozens of monitors
  • A reverse proxy (Caddy or nginx) in front of other boxes
  • One Docker container at a time, comfortably

See our RAM sizing guide for the full workload table and the thresholds where 1 GB stops being enough.

Where $5 stops being enough

  • Plex or Jellyfin — media transcoding needs CPU and RAM a $5 box doesn't have. See whether a VPS makes sense for Plex at all.
  • Multiple heavy apps at once — two Dockerized apps with their own DBs will blow 1 GB fast.
  • A serious database (Postgres under real load). Budget a 2 GB box at minimum.
  • Local AI agents — a 1 GB box can't run a meaningful local model. That belongs on a bigger box or a dedicated agent host.
