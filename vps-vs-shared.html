VPS vs Shared Hosting: When the Upgrade Actually Pays Off

Shared hosting is cheap until it costs you traffic. Here's the decision framework: upgrade to a VPS when your site's growth is being throttled by the host, not when you're just "getting bigger."

What's actually different

On shared hosting you share CPU, RAM, and IO with hundreds of neighbors — one noisy neighbor can slow you down even if your traffic is low. A VPS gives you a dedicated slice (or whole core). The trade is management: you own updates, security, backups.

The real trigger

Don't upgrade because a calculator says so. Upgrade when you see: pages that took 2s starting to take 6s at peak, or a host that throttles your site after hitting a "soft limit." That's throttling reading as shared-host oversell — and it's the moment a cheap VPS stops being a luxury and starts being the revenue-preserving move.

When shared is genuinely fine

A static portfolio, a low-traffic blog, or anything under ~10k visitors can stay on shared and save the management hassle. There's no shame in the cheap plan if it isn't the bottleneck.
