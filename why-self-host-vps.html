Why People Self-Host on a VPS (2026)

The real reasons — privacy, cost, control — and when it's not worth it.

Bottom line: most people who self-host on a VPS are doing it for one of three reasons: escaping per-seat SaaS fees, keeping data off someone else's roadmap, or wanting tools SaaS won't sell you. At $5–10/mo it's cheaper than most SaaS subscriptions and you own the whole stack.

The three big reasons

  • Cost. A $5 VPS replaces $10–40/mo of SaaS. Run your own n8n, uptime monitor, or Home Assistant for pennies.
  • Control & privacy. Your data stays on hardware you rent and admin yourself, not inside someone else's product roadmap.
  • Automation. A VPS lets you run AI agents and background automation that no closed SaaS exposes.

When it's NOT worth it

Don't self-host just to save $3. If you need a dead-simple website or a tool you'll use twice a month, the admin overhead isn't worth it. Match the tool to the task — see VPS vs shared hosting and WordPress on VPS vs shared.

Start small

You don't need a big box. A $5 VPS runs most single-app setups. Pick a provider, harden it in 10 minutes, and deploy one Docker stack at a time.
